# `src/eboot/` — not covered by an open source licence

The files in this directory are a decompilation of *The Sims 2* for PSP, a
commercial game published by Maxis / Electronic Arts.  They were recovered by
reverse engineering `EBOOT.BIN`, which ships with its symbol table stripped.

**They are therefore a derivative work of EA's copyrighted game, and the MIT
licence at the root of this repository does not apply to them.**  The root
`LICENSE` covers the tooling only.

Nobody can grant you rights to this material — not the author of the tooling,
and not the author of this file.  Those rights belong to EA.  What this
repository grants you is everything else it can:

* **Permission to read it.**  You may study it, quote from it, refer to it in
  your own writing, and use it to understand the game.
* **Permission to read the tooling.**  The MIT licence covers `tools/` and
  `include/` without restriction, including commercial use.
* **Permission to contribute tooling improvements.**  Pull requests that touch
  only `tools/`, `include/` or the pipeline are straightforward.

## What you need to do anything else with it

**You need your own copy of the game.**  No disc image, no decrypted
executable and no toolchain binary is committed to this repository; see
`.gitignore`.  `tools/setup.py` takes a path to a disc image you supply.

Build anything from this — a port, a mod, a reimplementation — and you need to
own that copy.  That is the ordinary condition on reverse engineering for
interoperability and study, and it is the condition under which this work was
done.

## If you want to contribute

Corrections, better names, and additional decompiled functions are welcome.  A
contribution here is a contribution of reverse-engineered material, so please
make sure you hold your own copy of the game and are comfortable with that.  It
also helps to say so in the pull request; it is not a legal formality, it is how
the next reader knows what they are looking at.

Contributions to `tools/` carry no such condition.

## A note on the licence choice

The reference project this one takes its structure from,
[tclamb/mhp2g-decomp](https://github.com/tclamb/mhp2g-decomp), uses CC0 at its
root.  CC0 is a public domain dedication: it asserts that the licensor has the
right to relinquish copyright entirely.  That assertion cannot honestly be made
about reverse-engineered game code, and a licence that overclaims is worse than
no licence, because it invites people to rely on a dedication that was never
valid.

So the tooling is MIT, which is a grant rather than a relinquishment, and the
recovered code is left unlicensed on purpose.  See also the *Legal* section of
the root `README.md`.
